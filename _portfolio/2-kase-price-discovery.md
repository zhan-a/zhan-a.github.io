Several Kazakh companies trade both on the Kazakhstan Stock Exchange and abroad. This project measures how much of a foreign price move has already arrived by the time KASE opens, and how quickly the rest follows.

## The identification

The usual difficulty in cross-listing studies is that the two venues trade at the same time, so it is hard to say which one led. Here the data contains a natural experiment. Kaspi.kz trades on KASE and NASDAQ with **no session overlap**: Almaty closes long before New York opens. Kazatomprom and Air Astana trade on KASE and the London Stock Exchange with a **four-hour overlap**. The venue, the currency, the broker and the cost structure are held fixed across the comparison; only the overlap changes.

Standard information-share measures assume the two venues are observed at the same time, which the no-overlap pair violates by construction. The strict sequencing is treated as an identifying advantage rather than a defect, which places the work in the spillover literature instead.

## What it finds

After foreign moves of 2% or more, about 81% of the day's KASE move is already in place half an hour after the open. The economic conclusion is negative and stated as such: this measures how information travels, not a trading opportunity. By the time an ordinary participant can act, what is left of the move is smaller than the cost of a round trip.
