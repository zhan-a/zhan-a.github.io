Several Kazakh companies trade both on the Kazakhstan Stock Exchange and abroad. This project measures how much of a foreign price move has already arrived by the time KASE opens, and how quickly the rest follows.

## The identification

The usual difficulty in cross-listing studies is that the two venues trade at the same time, so it is hard to say which one led. Here the data contains a natural experiment. Kaspi.kz trades on KASE and NASDAQ with **zero session overlap** — Almaty closes long before New York opens. Kazatomprom and Air Astana trade on KASE and the LSE with a **four-hour overlap**. The venue, the currency, the broker and the cost structure are held fixed across the comparison; only the overlap changes.

Standard information-share measures assume the two venues are observed contemporaneously, which the zero-overlap pair violates by construction. The strict sequencing is treated as an identifying advantage rather than a defect, positioning the work against the spillover literature instead.

## What it finds

Transmission of β = 0.20 at the open and 0.27 intraday across 590 sessions, with roughly 81% of the drift completing within the first 30 minutes. The result survives alternative FX construction, sub-period splits, an out-of-sample period, and a placebo pair that shows essentially nothing.

The economic conclusion is negative and stated as such: the effect is a measurement of how information travels, not a trading opportunity. By the time an ordinary participant could act, the hit rate is indistinguishable from a coin flip.
